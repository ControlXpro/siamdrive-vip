Bangkok District · Ratchadaphisek & Rama 9

Ratchada, From Boardroom to Night Market

Ratchadaphisek Road is a broad avenue running north from Asok, with the MRT beneath it and a fast-growing business district around the Rama 9 junction. By night it becomes one of Bangkok's busiest areas for street food, concerts and entertainment.

Wide roads, long U-turns

Ratchada looks easy to drive: several lanes in each direction and long, straight stretches. In practice, crossing to the other side often means travelling a considerable distance to the next U-turn or flyover, and the junctions at Rama 9 and Huai Khwang back up heavily in the evening.

A chauffeur who knows the area chooses the right side of the road from the outset and uses the service lanes and back sois where they help. It sounds like a small thing, but on Ratchada it can save twenty minutes on a single journey.

The road is also lined with large entertainment venues and office complexes that have their own drop-off arrangements. Your chauffeur will know where to stop and where to wait, which matters when security staff are keeping driveways clear.

Rama 9: the new business district

Around the Rama 9 MRT station, glass towers such as G Tower have brought regional offices and hotels to the area, joined by the large Central Rama 9 shopping centre. Many visiting executives now have meetings here as well as in Sukhumvit or Silom.

Moving between these districts is where a chauffeur pays for itself. The expressway connects Rama 9 to the rest of the city, and your driver can judge whether it or the surface roads will be quicker at that moment, while you prepare for the next meeting in a quiet cabin.

Hotels around Rama 9 and Ratchada are also popular with visitors attending events at the nearby Queen Sirikit National Convention Centre, which is a short run down Ratchadaphisek Road from here.

Ratchada after dark

Huai Khwang is known for its late-night street food, with stalls serving into the small hours. The Thailand Cultural Centre hosts concerts, ballet and theatre, and Esplanade Ratchada and The Street Ratchada add cinemas, restaurants and late-opening shops. Night markets in the area have come and gone over the years, so we will check what is open during your stay.

With an hourly booking, your chauffeur waits while you eat, watch or browse and brings the car to the entrance afterwards. When a concert ends and thousands of people look for taxis at once, that is a very welcome arrangement.

Choosing your service

A ten-hour chauffeur suits a working day split between Rama 9 and other districts. A five-hour booking covers an evening concert or dinner. Don Mueang is usually within easy reach via the tollway, and Suvarnabhumi via the expressway. Guests attending busy events can add a bodyguard, and a personal assistant can manage tickets, reservations and timings.

Local tips

  • Avoid crossing Ratchada at the Rama 9 junction between 5 and 8 pm.
  • After concerts, agree a side-street meeting point to skip the crowd.
  • Tell your chauffeur which side of Ratchada your venue faces.
  • Huai Khwang's street food is best after 8 pm, when the stalls are busiest.
