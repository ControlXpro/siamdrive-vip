Ayutthaya, the Ancient Capital

For more than four centuries, Ayutthaya was the capital of the Siamese kingdom and one of the great cities of Asia. Its temple ruins, now a UNESCO World Heritage Site, lie about an hour and a half north of Bangkok.

A royal summer palace

Begin at the Bang Pa-In Royal Palace, on the way to Ayutthaya. This elegant complex of European, Chinese and Thai-style buildings sits around ornamental lakes and gardens, and is a beautiful place to stroll in the morning.

Bang Pa-In has a strict dress code: shoulders and knees must be covered, and visitors in shorts or sleeveless tops may be refused entry or asked to rent appropriate clothing.

The palace grounds are best enjoyed slowly. Look out for the Chinese-style Wehart Chamrun hall, the elegant pavilion in the middle of the lake and the observatory tower, which offers views across the gardens.

The temples of the island city

Ayutthaya's historic centre sits on an island ringed by rivers. At Wat Mahathat, you will find the famous Buddha head entwined in the roots of a fig tree, one of Thailand's most photographed sights. Please keep your head lower than the Buddha's when taking photos.

Nearby, Wat Phra Si Sanphet's three great chedis once stood within the royal palace grounds, and the neighbouring Wihan Phra Mongkhon Bophit houses one of Thailand's largest bronze Buddha images.

The temples are spread out, unshaded and hot by late morning. Your chauffeur drives you between them and waits at each entrance with cold water and air-conditioning, so you see far more without exhausting yourself.

Ayutthaya was sacked in 1767, and the ruins you see today bear the scars of that destruction: headless Buddha images, toppled chedis and weathered brick. It is a moving place, and many visitors find it the most memorable part of their time in Thailand.

Lunch by the river

Ayutthaya is famous for grilled giant river prawns, served at riverside restaurants around the island. It is a memorable lunch and a chance to rest before the afternoon.

Afterwards, visit Wat Chaiwatthanaram, a majestic Khmer-style temple on the west bank of the Chao Phraya, especially atmospheric in the softer afternoon light.

If time allows, Wat Yai Chai Mongkhon, with its tall chedi and rows of saffron-draped Buddha images, and the reclining Buddha at Wat Lokayasutharam are both worthwhile additions. Your chauffeur can fit them in on the way back to the main road.

Why a private chauffeur

Group tours follow fixed timetables and often spend long periods at shopping stops. With a private chauffeur, you choose how long to stay at each temple, add or skip sites and set your own pace.

A ten-hour booking covers the full day, including travel. A personal assistant can act as guide and translator, explaining the history, and families appreciate the space of an Alphard after long walks in the heat.

Good to know

  • Cover shoulders and knees, especially for Bang Pa-In Palace.
  • Keep your head lower than the Buddha head at Wat Mahathat for photos.
  • Bring a hat, sunscreen and comfortable shoes; the ruins are unshaded.
  • Visit Wat Chaiwatthanaram late in the day for softer light.
