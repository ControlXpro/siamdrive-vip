An Evening of Bangkok Fine Dining

Bangkok has become one of Asia's great dining capitals, with chefs reinterpreting Thai cooking alongside world-class European tables. This evening pairs an aperitif, a tasting menu and a nightcap, with a chauffeur taking care of every journey in between.

Begin with an aperitif

Start at the Bamboo Bar at the Mandarin Oriental, a riverside institution with live jazz, or at a hotel bar of your choice. It is the perfect place to settle into the evening while the city's traffic eases.

Your chauffeur drops you at the hotel entrance and waits nearby. When it is time for dinner, the car is ready, so you never arrive flustered or late for a table that may have taken weeks to secure.

If you are staying on the Sukhumvit side, a hotel bar closer to your restaurant works just as well. Tell us where you are dining and we will suggest an aperitif stop that keeps the evening's driving short.

Choosing your table

Bangkok's Michelin Guide includes a remarkable range of restaurants. Some reinterpret regional Thai cooking, such as Sorn, with its focus on Southern Thai cuisine, or Nusara, facing Wat Pho in the Old Town. Others are progressive, such as Gaggan Anand, or rooted in European traditions, such as Le Normandie at the Mandarin Oriental or Côte at Capella Bangkok. Potong, in a restored Chinatown shophouse, blends Thai and Chinese influences.

Many of these restaurants are booked well in advance. Our personal assistant service can help request reservations, note dietary requirements and coordinate timings, though availability is always at each restaurant's discretion.

Tasting menus can last three hours or more, and the best dining rooms are spread across the city, from the riverside to Sukhumvit and the Old Town. That is exactly why a chauffeur is so useful on a night like this.

A nightcap to finish

After dinner, continue to one of the city's acclaimed cocktail bars. BKK Social Club at the Four Seasons Hotel Bangkok at Chao Phraya River is elegant and polished, while the small bars around Soi Nana in Chinatown offer a more intimate atmosphere.

Your chauffeur will take you there and then home, so you can enjoy the wine pairing without a second thought about how you will get back.

Dress and timing

Most fine-dining restaurants ask for smart casual dress. Men may prefer closed shoes and long trousers, and some venues discourage shorts and sandals. Arrive on time, as tasting menus are often served to a schedule.

Choose a five-hour booking for a single restaurant and nightcap, or add hours if you plan to linger. For a celebration, the Rowen Vellfire Z or Porsche Cayenne S makes a memorable arrival, and a discreet bodyguard can accompany high-profile guests.
