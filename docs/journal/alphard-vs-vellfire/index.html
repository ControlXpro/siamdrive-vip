Alphard vs Vellfire: Twins With Different Personalities

Bangkok’s favourite VIP vans are siblings under the skin. Here is how the Alphard and Vellfire differ in styling, cabin and character — and which one suits your journey.

One platform, two characters

Spend a few minutes on any five-star hotel forecourt in Bangkok and you will see them: the tall, slab-sided Toyota vans that have become the city’s default VIP car. The Alphard and the Vellfire are siblings, built on the same underpinnings with the same essential cabin layout, powertrains and dimensions. Mechanically, the differences are slight. The distinction is mostly one of character.

Toyota has long positioned the Alphard as the more formal, stately model, with a broad chrome grille and the bearing of an executive car. The Vellfire has traditionally been the bolder sibling, with darker trim, a more assertive front end and a slightly sportier attitude. The current generation continues that split, and in many markets the Vellfire is offered in a narrower range of trims with a more focused, darker look. Think of them as the same tailored suit, cut in two different fabrics.

Generations: the 30 and the 40

Chauffeur companies usually refer to the vans by generation. The ‘30’ series is the previous generation, sold from the mid-2010s, and it remains an excellent car: comfortable captain’s chairs in the second row, generous luggage space with the third row folded and a well-proven reputation for reliability in Bangkok’s heat and traffic.

The ‘40’ series is the current generation, launched in 2023. It brings a stiffer body, a noticeably quieter cabin, a more settled ride and an updated interior with better materials and technology. For most passengers the step from 30 to 40 shows up as refinement — less road noise at expressway speeds, calmer progress over Bangkok’s patched surfaces and a cabin that feels contemporary rather than merely comfortable. Both are genuinely premium; the 40 is simply the newer and quieter of the two.

Where a Rowen Vellfire fits

Rowen is a Japanese styling house known for aero kits and detailing for premium Japanese cars. A Rowen-styled Vellfire wears a distinctive body kit, grille and finishing that give the van far more road presence than a standard model. Underneath, it is the same comfortable Toyota platform, with the same seats and the same quiet ride.

It is the choice when arrival matters as much as the journey itself: weddings, product launches, gala evenings or a guest who simply appreciates something out of the ordinary. Those who would rather blend into the traffic will generally be happier in a standard Alphard in a dark finish, which looks smart at any hotel door without drawing a second glance.

The Executive Lounge cabin

Beyond styling sits a more practical question: which seats. Executive Lounge versions of the Alphard replace the standard second-row chairs with deeper, more adjustable seats — power recline, extending leg rests and individual controls — closer in spirit to a business-class seat than a car seat.

  • Standard luxury cabin: captain’s chairs and generous legroom, ideal for families, couples and small groups on city journeys.
  • Executive Lounge: reclining lounge seats with ottomans, suited to long transfers, jet-lagged arrivals and working or resting en route.
  • Third row: useful for extra passengers on short trips, though folding it frees up valuable luggage space.

Living with them in Bangkok

Part of the reason these vans dominate the city is that they suit its conditions so well. Sliding doors on both sides make kerbside stops quick and graceful, which matters on narrow sois and at busy hotel entrances where a car may only pause for a moment. The high roofline lets passengers step in rather than fold themselves down, a small mercy in formal clothes or after a long flight.

The cabins are also built for waiting. In stop-start traffic, a quiet, well-cooled interior with reading lights, power outlets and room to stretch turns an hour on Sukhumvit into usable time for calls, email or rest. Dark privacy glass keeps the afternoon sun at bay and lends a welcome degree of anonymity. None of this is unique to one badge or the other; it is simply why both have become the reference point for chauffeured travel across the region.

How to choose

A useful rule of thumb is to choose by passengers and luggage first, and by mood second. Around four travellers with four large cases sit comfortably in any of these vans; beyond that, consider a second vehicle or a separate car for luggage. Golf bags, prams and oversized cases all count, so mention them early.

Then decide on the impression you want. Discretion points to a dark Alphard 40; presence points to the Rowen Vellfire; maximum personal comfort, particularly on a three-hour run to Hua Hin or straight from an overnight flight to a meeting, points to the Executive Lounge. If you are torn, describe the trip — who is travelling, how far and what the day involves — in a short message on WhatsApp, and the team can suggest the most suitable vehicle.

Questions, answered

Anything else? Our concierge replies on WhatsApp, day or night.

Is the Vellfire bigger than the Alphard?

No. They share the same platform and essentially the same dimensions and cabin space. The differences lie in styling, trim and the overall character of the car.

How many passengers and bags fit in an Alphard?

Typically up to five passengers with around four large suitcases. Exact capacity depends on bag sizes and whether the third row is in use, so share your luggage count when booking.

Is the Executive Lounge worth it for a short airport run?

For short city journeys the standard cabin is excellent. The lounge seats come into their own on long transfers, after long-haul flights or when you plan to rest or work on the move.
