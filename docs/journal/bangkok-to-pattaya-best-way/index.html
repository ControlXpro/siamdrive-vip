Bangkok to Pattaya: Choosing the Best Way There

Around two hours down the motorway, Pattaya is Bangkok’s closest beach escape. How the main options compare, when to leave, and where to pause on the way.

The journey at a glance

Pattaya sits on the Gulf of Thailand, roughly 150 kilometres south-east of central Bangkok. By road the journey typically takes around two to two and a half hours, most of it on Motorway 7, a well-kept toll road that bypasses the slower coastal towns. From Suvarnabhumi Airport, which lies on the eastern side of the city, it is closer still — around 125 kilometres and often an hour and a half to two hours.

That proximity makes Pattaya unusually flexible. It works as a weekend at the beach, a golf day, a business trip to the Eastern Economic Corridor or a direct transfer from a long-haul flight straight to a resort in Jomtien or Wong Amat.

Your options compared

Several ways of making the trip exist, and each suits a different traveller.

  • Private car with chauffeur: door to door, on your schedule, with space for luggage and the freedom to stop. The most comfortable choice for families, groups and arrivals from the airport.
  • Taxi: widely available, though vehicle quality varies and long-distance fares are usually agreed in advance rather than metered. Large cases can be a squeeze in a standard saloon.
  • Coach: scheduled services run from Bangkok’s Eastern Bus Terminal at Ekkamai and from Suvarnabhumi. Inexpensive and reasonably comfortable, but you will need onward transport to your hotel.
  • Ride-hailing apps: convenient for booking, with varying vehicles and drivers on longer journeys.
  • Train: a slow daily rail service exists and is scenic, but its timings rarely suit visitors.

Timing your departure

The drive itself is straightforward; leaving Bangkok is the variable. On weekdays, departing before around 7am or after 10am avoids the worst of the morning rush on the city’s eastern arteries. Friday afternoons and evenings are the slowest time to head south-east, as residents leave for the weekend, while Sunday afternoons bring a matching wave of traffic back into the city.

Long weekends and public holidays amplify both effects, and the days immediately before Songkran in mid-April are among the busiest of the year on every road out of Bangkok. If your dates are flexible, a mid-week trip is markedly calmer in both directions.

Weather is the other wildcard. During the rainy season, roughly May to October, heavy afternoon downpours can slow traffic sharply on the way out of the city, even if the motorway beyond is clear. An early start sidesteps both the storms, which tend to build later in the day, and the heat, leaving the afternoon free for the beach or the pool.

Making the most of the drive

Because the motorway is fast, many travellers treat the journey as a simple transfer. With a private car, though, it is easy to make a pleasant half-day of it. Bang Saen, a breezy beach town popular with Thai families, is roughly an hour and a half from central Bangkok, and Sriracha, a little further on, is known for seafood restaurants over the water.

In and around Pattaya itself, the carved-teak Sanctuary of Truth and the gardens at Nong Nooch reward an unhurried visit. A ten-hour hire allows a full day on the coast and a relaxed drive home after dinner, without anyone needing to check a bus timetable or haggle over a fare at the end of the evening.

Knowing where you are staying

Pattaya is longer than many first-time visitors expect, and the right drop-off point shapes the final half-hour of the journey. To the north, Naklua and Wong Amat are quieter, with several of the larger resort hotels set along a calmer stretch of sand. Central Pattaya, around Beach Road and Second Road, is the busy heart of the city, where evening traffic can slow to a crawl. South of the headland, Jomtien and Na Jomtien are more residential and relaxed, popular with families and longer-stay visitors.

Share the exact hotel or villa address in advance, particularly for private residences in the hills behind the coast, where satellite navigation is not always reliable. If you are flying in or out of U-Tapao, the smaller airport south of Pattaya, the drive from most hotels is usually well under an hour, which makes it a convenient alternative for some regional flights.

Choosing the right car

Couples and solo travellers with modest luggage are well served by an executive sedan such as the Toyota Camry. Families, groups of friends and anyone with golf bags — the hills around Pattaya hold some of Thailand’s best-known courses — will appreciate the space of an Alphard, where captain’s chairs and a high roofline make two hours pass easily.

Whichever you choose, let the driver know your hotel, any planned stops and your luggage count in advance. To arrange a transfer or a full day on the coast, send your dates and party size on WhatsApp and the team will confirm availability.

Questions, answered

Anything else? Our concierge replies on WhatsApp, day or night.

How long does it take to drive from Bangkok to Pattaya?

Typically around two to two and a half hours from central Bangkok, depending on traffic when leaving the city. Friday evenings and holiday periods can add considerably to that.

Can I go straight from Suvarnabhumi Airport to Pattaya?

Yes. Suvarnabhumi is on the eastern side of Bangkok, so a direct transfer avoids the city altogether and usually takes around an hour and a half to two hours.

Can we stop along the way?

With a private car, yes. Short stops for refreshments or a meal at Bang Saen or Sriracha are easy to add; mention them when booking so the timing can be planned.
