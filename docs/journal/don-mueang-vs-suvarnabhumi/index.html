Don Mueang vs Suvarnabhumi: Bangkok’s Two Airports Compared

Bangkok has two major airports, and booking the wrong one is an easy mistake. Which airlines fly from where, how far each is from the city and how to connect between them.

Two airports, two roles

Bangkok is served by two large international airports, and the difference between them matters more than many visitors realise. Suvarnabhumi (BKK), opened in 2006, is the city’s principal international gateway: a vast, modern terminal around 30 kilometres east of the centre, handling most long-haul and full-service traffic.

Don Mueang (DMK), around 25 kilometres to the north, is the older of the two and one of the longest-serving airports in Asia. After Suvarnabhumi opened it found a second life as Bangkok’s low-cost hub, and today it is busy with domestic and regional flights. The two are roughly 45 kilometres apart, on opposite sides of the city, so confusing them on the day of travel can be a costly error.

It happens more often than you might think: a traveller books a hotel near one airport for an early flight, only to discover on the morning of departure that the flight leaves from the other. Checking the three-letter code on each booking is the simplest safeguard of all.

Which airlines use which

As a broad guide, the split looks like this — though schedules change, and some carriers operate from both airports, so always check the airport code on your ticket.

  • Don Mueang (DMK): mostly low-cost carriers, including Thai AirAsia, Nok Air and Thai Lion Air, plus a number of regional budget airlines.
  • Suvarnabhumi (BKK): Thai Airways, Bangkok Airways and the major international full-service carriers from Europe, the Middle East, Asia and Australia, along with some low-cost and long-haul budget services.
  • Domestic connections: if you are flying onward to Phuket, Chiang Mai or Koh Samui, your departure airport depends on the airline rather than the destination.

Getting to and from the city

Suvarnabhumi is connected to central Bangkok by the Airport Rail Link and by the expressway network to the east, which makes it particularly convenient for Sukhumvit, Bang Na and onward trips to Pattaya and the Eastern Seaboard. Don Mueang is reached by the elevated Don Mueang Tollway and by the SRT Red Line, and is often the quicker option for northern districts such as Ari, Chatuchak and Ratchada.

By car, both airports typically take around thirty minutes to an hour to reach from central Bangkok, depending on traffic. Neither is reliably faster than the other; the deciding factors are where you are staying and the time of day.

Late at night and in the early hours, both airports are quick to reach and the choice of airport makes little practical difference. Just as many international flights land between midnight and dawn, however, public transport options thin out considerably, and a pre-arranged car becomes the simplest way to reach your hotel without queuing for a taxi after a long flight.

Connecting between the two

Travellers on separate tickets — arriving on a long-haul flight into Suvarnabhumi, say, and continuing on a budget airline from Don Mueang — face one of the more stressful transfers in the region. You will usually need to clear immigration, collect your luggage, cross the city and check in again from scratch.

The road journey between the airports typically takes around 45 minutes to an hour and a quarter outside the peaks, and considerably longer in rush hour or heavy rain. A free shuttle bus has operated between the airports for passengers holding an onward ticket, but it runs to a timetable and can be crowded. Many experienced travellers allow at least four to five hours between flights on separate tickets, and a private transfer removes one of the main variables: waiting.

What each terminal is like

Suvarnabhumi is enormous. The walk from a remote gate to immigration can be long, queues at peak times can be substantial, and it is here that fast-track services, electric buggies and premium lounges make the greatest difference. In return, it offers a wide choice of airline lounges, dining and shopping.

Don Mueang is more compact but often very busy, with fewer premium facilities. Low-cost carriers tend to enforce check-in deadlines and baggage allowances strictly, so arriving in good time and weighing bags in advance can save both money and stress.

Both airports have international and domestic sections, and at Suvarnabhumi in particular these can be a long walk apart. When you book a car or a greeter, mention whether your flight is international or domestic, so the meeting point is set at the right end of the terminal from the outset.

Planning your transfer

Whichever airport you use, give your driver the flight number rather than just a time, so arrivals can be tracked and departures planned around realistic traffic. For connections between airports, share both flight numbers. If you would like a transfer arranged to or from either airport, send your details on WhatsApp and the team will confirm the plan.

How far apart are Don Mueang and Suvarnabhumi?

Around 45 kilometres by road, on opposite sides of Bangkok. The drive typically takes 45 minutes to an hour and a quarter off-peak, and longer in heavy traffic.

Can I land at one airport and fly out of the other on the same day?

Yes, but on separate tickets you will usually need to clear immigration, collect bags and check in again. Many travellers allow at least four to five hours between flights.

Which airport is closer to Sukhumvit?

Suvarnabhumi is generally more convenient for Sukhumvit and the eastern side of the city, while Don Mueang suits northern areas such as Ari and Chatuchak. Traffic often matters more than distance.
