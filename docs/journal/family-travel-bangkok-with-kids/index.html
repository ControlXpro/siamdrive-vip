Bangkok With Children: A Calmer Way to Travel

Bangkok can be wonderful with children, provided the logistics are right. Child seats, sensible pacing, days out that work and how to make arrival day easy.

Better with children than you might expect

Bangkok can seem an intimidating prospect for a family holiday: heat, traffic, crowds and a scale that dwarfs most European cities. Yet many parents find it one of the easiest cities in Asia to travel with children. Thai culture is famously warm towards young ones, hotels are generous with family rooms and pools, and the great air-conditioned malls offer aquariums, play areas and food courts that rescue many a rainy afternoon.

The challenges are real but manageable. Distances between sights are long, the midday heat is draining, and small travellers tire quickly in traffic. The families who enjoy Bangkok most tend to share two habits: they get the transport right, and they plan days at a child’s pace rather than an adult’s.

Getting the transport right

Child seats are the first priority. Thailand has strengthened its rules on child restraints in vehicles in recent years, and in any case a properly fitted seat is simply the safe choice. Request seats in advance, giving each child’s age and approximate weight, so the right type — infant carrier, convertible seat or booster — is installed before you set off. Bringing your own is also an option on longer stays.

Space matters nearly as much. A luxury MPV such as the Alphard, with sliding doors, captain’s chairs and room for a folded pushchair, makes the in-and-out rhythm of a family day far easier than a sedan. Door-to-door transport also means no waiting on hot pavements, no negotiating fares with tired children in tow and a cool, quiet place to rest between stops. If your stay is longer, having the same driver each day gives children a familiar face.

Days out that work

The city has plenty to occupy children, and many of the best options are indoors — useful in the heat and the rainy season. Opening hours and age suitability vary, so check before you go.

  • SEA LIFE Bangkok Ocean World: a large aquarium beneath Siam Paragon, easily combined with lunch in the mall.
  • KidZania Bangkok: a role-play city where children try out grown-up jobs, also at Siam Paragon.
  • Safari World: a drive-through safari park and marine park on the north-eastern edge of the city, best as a half or full day.
  • Lumphini Park: open space, boating and monitor lizards, ideally early in the morning before the heat builds.
  • A Chao Phraya boat ride: river views, temples and the bustle of the waterfront, with ICONSIAM’s indoor floating market on the far bank.
  • Dream World: an amusement park to the north, popular with older children.

Pacing, heat and health

The single most useful rule is to plan outdoor activities for the early morning and late afternoon, and to retreat indoors — or to the hotel pool — through the hottest hours. Two main activities a day is often plenty. Keep water, sun cream, hats and a change of clothes in the car, and do not underestimate how quickly children become dehydrated in Bangkok’s humidity.

Car journeys can double as nap time if you time them well; a sleeping toddler in a cool, quiet cabin is a gift to any parent. It is also reassuring to know that Bangkok has a number of internationally accredited private hospitals with English-speaking staff, should anything unexpected arise.

Food is rarely a problem. Thai cooking can be adapted for younger palates, with mild dishes such as fried rice, grilled chicken and clear soups on most menus, and the malls offer familiar international options for the fussiest eaters. Asking for a dish to be made without chilli is perfectly normal.

Arrival day with little ones

Arrival is where families feel the strain most. After a long flight, the walk to immigration at Suvarnabhumi can be lengthy, and queues at peak times test everyone’s patience. Fast-track services, with a greeter at the gate and an electric buggy where available, make a real difference here, as does a porter for the luggage mountain that families inevitably travel with.

Ask for the car to be ready with child seats fitted, water on board and the air-conditioning already running. Share your luggage count, including pushchairs and car seats, so the right vehicle is allocated. The aim is a seamless line from aircraft door to hotel room, with as little waiting in between as possible.

Day trips beyond the city

Once everyone has settled in, a day out of the city can be a highlight. The floating markets to the south-west, the ruins of Ayutthaya to the north and the beaches of Pattaya and Bang Saen are all within a couple of hours’ drive, and a private car allows for snack stops, toilet breaks and a flexible return. To plan transport for your family’s stay, send your dates, the children’s ages and your hotel on WhatsApp and the team will suggest a suitable vehicle and set-up.

Can child seats be provided in the car?

Yes, on request. Share each child’s age and approximate weight when booking so the appropriate infant seat, child seat or booster can be fitted in advance.

Which vehicle suits a family of five with luggage?

A luxury MPV such as the Toyota Alphard typically suits up to five passengers with around four large cases. If you have pushchairs or extra bags, mention them when booking.

Is Bangkok a safe city for children?

Bangkok is generally welcoming and safe for families. The main considerations are practical: heat, hydration, traffic and pacing the day to suit younger travellers.
