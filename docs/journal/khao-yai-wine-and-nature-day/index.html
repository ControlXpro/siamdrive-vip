A Khao Yai Day of Wine and Nature

Cooler air, forested hills, waterfalls and vineyards, a little under three hours from Bangkok. How to shape a Khao Yai day that balances the park with a long lunch.

Bangkok’s highland escape

Around 180 kilometres north-east of Bangkok, the land rises into the forested hills of Khao Yai. The drive typically takes two and a half to three hours, and the change in atmosphere is striking: the air is noticeably cooler, the light softer, and the landscape a patchwork of jungle, pasture and vineyards.

Khao Yai National Park, established in 1962, is Thailand’s oldest national park and forms part of the Dong Phayayen–Khao Yai Forest Complex, a UNESCO World Heritage site. Outside the park boundary, the surrounding district has become a favourite weekend retreat for Bangkok residents, with boutique hotels, farm cafés and some of Thailand’s best-known wineries.

Shaping the day

A day trip is entirely possible, but it rewards a plan. The park is at its best in the morning, when wildlife is more active and the heat is gentler, while the wineries suit a long lunch and a late-afternoon tasting.

Resist the temptation to fit in everything. Khao Yai’s attractions are spread across a wide area, and the drive between the park’s main sights and the wineries can take longer than the map suggests. Choosing one area of the park and one or two estates usually makes for a more enjoyable day than a rushed circuit.

  • Around 6.30–7am: depart Bangkok to beat the morning traffic on the Mittraphap (Friendship) Highway.
  • Mid-morning: enter the national park for viewpoints, a short forest walk and Haew Suwat Waterfall.
  • Lunch: a winery restaurant overlooking the vines.
  • Afternoon: a cellar tour and tasting, or a second estate for comparison.
  • Late afternoon: coffee at a farm café, then the drive back to Bangkok, arriving in the evening.

In the national park

Private vehicles can drive into the park, and its well-surfaced but winding roads lead to visitor centres, viewpoints and trailheads. Haew Suwat Waterfall is the most visited, while the viewing towers overlooking open grassland offer a chance of spotting deer and, with luck, wild elephants in the early morning or late afternoon. Gibbons and hornbills are often heard before they are seen.

A few sensible precautions apply. Foreign visitors pay a higher entry fee than Thai nationals. Wild elephants sometimes walk along the park roads; drivers should keep a generous distance and never block their path. Do not feed the macaques, and in the rainy season consider leech socks for forest trails. Guided walks with park rangers or local naturalists add a great deal for those with a particular interest in wildlife.

The wineries

Khao Yai is one of the centres of what is sometimes called ‘new latitude’ wine — viticulture outside the traditional temperate belts, made possible here by altitude and a relatively cool, dry winter. Established estates such as PB Valley and GranMonte offer vineyard tours, tastings and restaurants, and the wines, particularly the whites and rosés, are well worth exploring with an open mind.

Tours often run at set times and fill up at weekends, so reserving ahead is wise. The great advantage of travelling with a chauffeur is obvious: everyone in the party can enjoy the tasting, and nobody has to face a winding mountain road or a busy highway afterwards.

Most estates also sell bottles at the cellar door, along with grape juice and local produce for those not drinking, so the journey home can include a few souvenirs for later.

When to go

The cool season, from roughly November to February, is the most popular time, with crisp mornings and clear skies; it is also the busiest, especially at weekends. The rainy season brings lush green landscapes and waterfalls at their most dramatic, though trails can be slippery and some viewpoints shrouded in cloud. The hot season, around March to May, is still pleasant at altitude compared with Bangkok.

Mid-week visits are far calmer, both in the park and on the highway. Weekend and long-holiday traffic on the Mittraphap Highway can add significantly to the journey, particularly on Sunday afternoons heading back to the city.

Day trip or overnight

Families often find the area particularly rewarding. Farm attractions, horse riding and open space make a welcome contrast with Bangkok’s malls, and a spacious vehicle with room for a nap on the way home makes a long day far easier for younger travellers.

A single day covers the highlights comfortably with an early start. If you would like to see the park at dawn, linger longer over dinner or combine wine with a spa stay, an overnight at one of the area’s resorts makes the trip more relaxed. FAQ

Questions, answered

Anything else? Our concierge replies on WhatsApp, day or night.

Is Khao Yai too far for a day trip from Bangkok?

No, but it is a long day. With the drive typically taking two and a half to three hours each way, an early start allows time for both the national park and a winery.

Can we visit the national park and a winery on the same day?

Yes. A common plan is the park in the morning, when wildlife is more active, followed by lunch and a tasting at a winery in the afternoon.

When is the best time of year to visit Khao Yai?

The cool season from roughly November to February is the most popular. The rainy season offers lush scenery and fuller waterfalls, and mid-week visits are calmer year-round.
