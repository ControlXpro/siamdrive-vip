A Monthly Private Driver in Bangkok, Explained

For executives, relocating families and long-stay visitors, a dedicated monthly driver brings continuity that daily bookings cannot. How it works and what to agree up front.

Who a monthly driver suits

For a few days in Bangkok, booking cars as you need them works perfectly well. Over weeks or months, the picture changes. A dedicated driver and vehicle, retained on a monthly basis, becomes part of the rhythm of daily life — the school run, the morning commute, client lunches, weekend excursions and the inevitable late-night airport pick-up.

The arrangement is popular with expatriate executives on assignment, families relocating to the city, regional directors who divide their time between Bangkok and neighbouring capitals, and long-stay visitors, including those in Thailand for extended medical treatment. What unites them is the value they place on reliability and on not having to think about transport at all.

It can also suit those who would never think of themselves as needing a chauffeur. A household with an elderly parent, a family whose children attend school across the city, or a small business whose directors spend much of the day meeting clients may all find that a monthly driver saves more time, and more stress, than any other single arrangement.

How a monthly arrangement typically works

Although the details differ from one arrangement to the next, most share a common structure: an agreed working pattern for the driver, a dedicated vehicle and a clear basis for anything outside the routine. It is worth setting these points out in writing at the start, so both sides know exactly what to expect.

  • Working pattern: the number of days per week and hours per day the driver is on duty.
  • Overtime: how hours beyond the agreed pattern — late dinners, early flights — are handled.
  • Running costs: whether fuel, expressway tolls and parking are included or settled separately.
  • Out-of-town trips: arrangements for journeys beyond Bangkok and for overnight stays.
  • Cover: what happens when the driver is on leave, unwell or on a public holiday.
  • Notice: the terms for extending, pausing or ending the arrangement.

Choosing the vehicle

For monthly arrangements, the Toyota Alphard is the natural choice, and both recent generations are well suited. The current Alphard 40 offers the quietest cabin and the most contemporary interior, ideal for an executive who treats the car as a mobile office. The previous-generation Alphard 30 remains comfortable, spacious and dependable, and is a sensible choice for family duties and daily errands.

Think about the typical week rather than the exceptional day. A family with two children, school bags and sports kit will appreciate the space of a van, while an executive travelling alone may value quiet and privacy above all. If the occasional event calls for something different, a second car can usually be added for a single day.

The value of continuity

The real advantage of a monthly driver is not the car but the person. Over the first few weeks, a good driver learns the details that make a household run smoothly: which entrance to use at the office tower, the drop-off rules at the international school, the shortcut that avoids a flooded soi in the rainy season, and how you like the cabin when you are working rather than resting.

Trust builds in the same way. Parents grow comfortable with the driver collecting children; executives speak freely on calls, confident in the driver’s discretion. A familiar, English-speaking face also helps newcomers settle into a city that can be bewildering in the first months, from finding a tailor to navigating a hospital appointment.

Monthly driver or your own car?

Some expatriates consider buying or leasing a car instead. Import duties make luxury vehicles in Thailand considerably more expensive than in many countries, and owning one brings insurance, servicing, registration and parking to manage. Driving yourself in Bangkok is also a commitment: the traffic is demanding, local road habits take time to learn, and a foreign licence may not be valid for long-term residents.

A monthly driver avoids all of that, and the arrangement can be scaled up for a busy quarter or paused while you are away. For many households, the flexibility alone outweighs any appeal of ownership.

Getting started

A short trial period is often a sensible way to begin, particularly for families. A few weeks are usually enough to see whether the driver, the vehicle and the working pattern suit your routine, and to fine-tune the details before committing to a longer term.

The best arrangements begin with a frank conversation about your routine: who will use the car, typical hours, regular destinations and the occasional exceptions. From there, a suitable vehicle and driver can be matched and terms agreed. To discuss a monthly arrangement, describe your typical week on WhatsApp and the team will come back with options.

FAQ

Questions, answered

Anything else? Our concierge replies on WhatsApp, day or night.

Will I have the same driver every day?

That is the aim of a monthly arrangement. Continuity is its main advantage, with a replacement driver provided when your regular driver is on leave or unwell.

Are fuel and expressway tolls included?

It depends on the arrangement. Running costs such as fuel, tolls and parking should be clearly agreed at the outset, so confirm what is included before you begin.

Can the driver handle late-night airport runs?

Yes, though hours outside the agreed working pattern are usually treated as overtime. Agree how early starts and late finishes are handled when setting up the arrangement.
