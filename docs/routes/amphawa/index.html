Amphawa, where the canals glow

Amphawa is the floating market Thais themselves love most: a riverside town of wooden houses, canal-side cafés and seafood grilled on boats. It comes alive on weekend afternoons and evenings, and ends with boat rides to see fireflies glittering in the trees.

The drive to Samut Songkhram

Amphawa lies in Samut Songkhram province, approximately 75 km south-west of Bangkok. Your chauffeur takes Rama II Road, Highway 35, through salt flats and fish farms, and the journey usually takes around an hour and a half.

Rama II can be slow at peak times, so we plan around the traffic. For an afternoon visit, leaving Bangkok around midday or early afternoon brings you into Amphawa as the market warms up.

An afternoon and evening market

Unlike Damnoen Saduak, Amphawa is at its best later in the day, and mainly from Friday to Sunday. Stalls line both banks of the canal, selling sweets, crafts and souvenirs, while boats moored at the steps grill prawns, squid and scallops to order.

As dusk falls, long-tail boats set off to look for fireflies in the lamphu trees along the river. How many you see depends on the season and the weather, but on a good evening the effect is enchanting. Many of these boat tours also pass illuminated riverside temples.

Between the stalls, look for the old wooden shophouses that have been turned into cafés and guesthouses, many with small balconies over the water. Finding a table there for a drink as the lanterns come on is one of the nicest moments of the day.

More to see nearby

Maeklong Railway Market, a few minutes away, is one of the most extraordinary sights in Thailand: a working market spread across the railway line, with awnings folded back for each passing train. Wat Bang Kung, whose small chapel is wrapped in banyan roots, and the King Rama II Memorial Park, celebrating the poet-king born nearby, are both worth a visit.

Don Hoi Lot, at the mouth of the Maeklong River, is known for its razor-clam beds and seafood restaurants, and is at its most interesting at low tide.

Ten hours, at your pace

Amphawa is priced on our Eastern Seaboard tier, which includes up to ten hours with the car and chauffeur. A natural plan is an early-afternoon departure, the railway market and Wat Bang Kung, the floating market at dusk and a firefly boat before the drive home.

Returning late at night is effortless with your own driver, and you can simply sleep in the back. If you would rather stay over in a riverside homestay, take the one-way option and we will collect you the next day. Book on WhatsApp.

Because the market is busiest on Saturday evenings, your chauffeur will drop you close to the canal and arrange a clear meeting point, so you are not searching for the car among the crowds afterwards.

Chauffeur's tips

  • Visit Friday to Sunday; on weekdays the market is very quiet.
  • Plan to arrive around 3pm and stay until after dark for the firefly boats.
  • Bring mosquito repellent for the evening boat ride.
  • Visit Don Hoi Lot at low tide to see the clam beds exposed.

FAQ

Questions, answered

Anything else? Our concierge replies on WhatsApp, day or night.

When is Amphawa Floating Market open?

It is mainly a Friday to Sunday market, busiest from mid-afternoon into the evening.

How far is Amphawa from Bangkok?

Approximately 75 km, usually around an hour and a half via Rama II Road.

Can we see fireflies every night?

Sightings vary with season and weather. Boat operators run trips most weekend evenings, and your chauffeur will wait until you return.

Can we combine Amphawa with Damnoen Saduak?

Yes. Start at Damnoen Saduak in the morning and finish at Amphawa in the evening, all within the ten-hour allowance.
