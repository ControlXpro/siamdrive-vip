Ayutthaya, the old capital at leisure

For more than four centuries, Ayutthaya was the capital of Siam and one of the great cities of Asia. Its temple ruins, now a UNESCO World Heritage Site, lie about an hour and a half north of Bangkok, and a private chauffeur is easily the most comfortable way to see them.

The drive north

Your chauffeur leaves the city on the elevated Don Mueang Tollway or Phahonyothin Road, Highway 1, continuing north past Rangsit and Bang Pa-in to Ayutthaya. The distance is approximately 80 km and the journey takes around an hour and a half, a little longer in morning rush hour.

Many visitors stop on the way at Bang Pa-in Palace, the royal summer residence with its lake, pavilions and an unusual mix of Thai, Chinese and European architecture. It is about 20 minutes short of Ayutthaya and works well as either the first or last stop of the day.

Temples worth your time

Ayutthaya's historical park spreads across an island ringed by rivers, with the main ruins too far apart to walk comfortably in the heat. Wat Mahathat is famous for the stone Buddha head entwined in tree roots, while the three great chedis of Wat Phra Si Sanphet stand beside the former royal palace grounds.

On the riverbank, Wat Chaiwatthanaram is perhaps the most photogenic of all, especially in the golden light of late afternoon. Wat Yai Chai Mongkhon, with its rows of saffron-robed Buddha images, and the vast reclining Buddha at Wat Lokayasutharam complete a classic circuit.

Why go with your own chauffeur

Organised coach tours follow a fixed timetable and often spend their best hours in transit. With a private car, you choose the order, linger where you like and retreat to a cool cabin between sites. Your chauffeur waits at each temple car park, so water, hats and shopping stay safely in the car.

Ayutthaya is priced on our Eastern Seaboard tier, which includes up to ten hours with the car. That is generous for a relaxed day with lunch by the river and a sunset at Wat Chaiwatthanaram before driving home.

Families appreciate the flexibility most. Children tire quickly in the heat, and being able to return to an air-conditioned Alphard for a rest between temples keeps the whole day pleasant.

Eating and resting

Ayutthaya is known for its river prawns, grilled and served at restaurants along the water, and for roti sai mai, thin pancakes wrapped around strands of spun sugar, sold from stalls around the town. A riverside lunch is a highlight of the day.

If you would prefer to see the temples from the water, long-tail boats circle the island in the late afternoon, and your chauffeur can meet you at the landing afterwards. Book on WhatsApp and tell us which sights matter most to you.

Chauffeur's tips

  • Leave Bangkok by 7.30am to see the first temples before the midday heat.
  • Cover shoulders and knees for temples and Bang Pa-in Palace, where dress codes are enforced.
  • Save Wat Chaiwatthanaram for late afternoon, when the light is at its best.
  • Carry small notes for temple entry fees and water from local vendors.
