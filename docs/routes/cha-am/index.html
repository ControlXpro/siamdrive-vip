Cha-am: the calmer side of the coast

Cha-am lies just north of Hua Hin and is where many Bangkok families escape for a weekend of seafood and sea breezes. It is a little closer, a little quieter and home to some of the region's largest beachfront resorts.

≈ 175 km2.5–3 hrsRoyal CoastFrom 16,000 THB

On the road south

The usual route follows Rama II Road out of Bangkok before joining Phetkasem Road, Highway 4, which carries you past Phetchaburi and straight into Cha-am. For approximately 175 km, allow around two and a half to three hours, a little more if you leave on a Friday afternoon.

Phetchaburi is the obvious place to break the journey. The hilltop palace of Phra Nakhon Khiri overlooks the town, and the nearby Tham Khao Luang cave, with its shafts of light falling on Buddha images, is a striking detour of under an hour.

Why travellers choose Cha-am

Cha-am's long beach is lined with casuarina trees, deckchairs and seafood stalls, and it feels distinctly Thai at weekends. Many international visitors use it as a peaceful base, with large resorts set on wide gardens and easy access to Hua Hin's restaurants about half an hour away.

For families, the area has a run of themed attractions along Phetkasem Road, including Santorini Park and the Swiss Sheep Farm. History lovers should not miss Maruekhathaiyawan Palace, an elegant seaside teak residence built for King Rama VI.

Golfers will find several well-regarded courses between Cha-am and Hua Hin, and the town's flat beach road is pleasant for an early walk or cycle before the heat arrives. Evenings are relaxed: a sunset drink, a long dinner and an early night are very much the local rhythm.

Multi-generational groups often book an Alphard or Vellfire for the space and the sliding doors, which make getting grandparents and small children in and out simple. If you have a lot of luggage, two cars travelling together is easily arranged on WhatsApp.

Quiet arrival, unhurried departure

Your chauffeur delivers you to the resort lobby and helps with bags to the door. On the way home, you can stop at a Phetchaburi dessert shop or a roadside fruit stall, a pleasure that rarely fits into a bus timetable.

Seafood is the real reason many Thais make this trip. Simple beachfront restaurants grill squid, prawns and whole fish over charcoal, and your chauffeur will happily point you towards a local favourite rather than a tourist menu. If you prefer to dine in Hua Hin one evening, a short return run along Highway 4 can be arranged on WhatsApp.

Chauffeur's tips

  • Weekends in Cha-am are busy with Thai visitors; weekdays are much quieter on the beach.
  • Visit Tham Khao Luang in late morning, when sunlight falls through the cave openings.
  • Leave Bangkok early on a Friday to stay ahead of the weekend exodus on Rama II.
  • Pick up Phetchaburi's famous khanom mo kaeng custard on the way home for a local treat.

How far is Cha-am from Bangkok?

Approximately 175 km, usually around two and a half to three hours by car depending on traffic.

Is Cha-am the same as Hua Hin?

They are neighbouring towns about 25 km apart. Cha-am is quieter and slightly closer to Bangkok, with Hua Hin's restaurants a short drive away.
