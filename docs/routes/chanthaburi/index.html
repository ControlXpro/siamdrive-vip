Chanthaburi: gems, orchards and history

Chanthaburi is one of the east's most rewarding and least crowded provinces, famous for its gem market, its fruit orchards and a French-era cathedral beside the river. It is an easy, scenic drive with a private chauffeur, and a fascinating place to spend a day or a weekend.

The road east

From Bangkok your chauffeur heads out on Motorway 7, continues past Laem Chabang on Highway 36 to bypass Pattaya, then joins Sukhumvit Road, Highway 3, through Rayong province to Chanthaburi. The distance is approximately 245 km, and the journey takes around three and a half to four hours.

The final stretch runs past durian and rambutan orchards and roadside stalls selling the season's fruit, so a brief stop is almost obligatory. Service areas on Motorway 7 and petrol-station cafés along Highway 3 make easy rest points.

If you set off early, you can reach Chanthaburi in time for a late breakfast at the riverside, explore the old quarter before the midday heat and spend the afternoon among the orchards or at the waterfall before heading home or onward to your hotel.

The gem market and the old town

Chanthaburi has been a centre of the gemstone trade for generations, and on weekends the streets around Sri Chan Road fill with dealers examining sapphires and rubies at folding tables. It is a remarkable sight even if you are not buying, and it is busiest from Friday to Sunday.

A short walk away, the Chanthaboon Waterfront community preserves wooden shophouses along the river, now home to cafés and small museums. Across the water stands the Cathedral of the Immaculate Conception, a Gothic Revival church often described as the largest Catholic cathedral in Thailand.

Orchards, waterfalls and coast

Fruit season, usually from around May to June, brings visitors to orchards that offer tastings of durian, mangosteen and longkong. Namtok Phlio National Park, a short drive from town, has a clear pool full of fish beneath a waterfall, and the mangrove bay of Khung Kraben has a long boardwalk and a well-presented learning centre.

With a chauffeur, all of these sit comfortably within one day, because you are never waiting for a songthaew or negotiating a return ride from a remote car park. Your chauffeur can also suggest a local restaurant for Chanthaburi's distinctive dishes, such as sen chan rice noodles or pork braised with chamuang leaves.
