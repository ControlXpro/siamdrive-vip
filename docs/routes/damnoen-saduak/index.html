Damnoen Saduak, before the crowds arrive

Damnoen Saduak is Thailand's most famous floating market, a maze of canals where vendors paddle boats piled with fruit, noodles and flowers. The secret to enjoying it is arriving early, and a private chauffeur is the simplest way to be there as the first boats set out.

≈ 100 km1.5–2 hrsWestern FrontierFrom 14,000 THB

An early start pays off

Damnoen Saduak lies approximately 100 km south-west of Bangkok, in Ratchaburi province. Your chauffeur usually takes Rama II Road or Phetkasem Road, Highway 4, depending on your pick-up point and the morning traffic, and the drive takes around one and a half to two hours.

The market is liveliest from early morning until late morning, after which the tour coaches arrive in force and the canals become crowded. Leaving Bangkok around 6.30am lets you board a boat at around 8am, when the light is soft and the vendors are setting up.

On the water

Visitors explore the canals by paddle boat or motorised long-tail, hired from the piers along the main canal. Boat prices are set by the operators rather than by us, and it is worth agreeing the duration and route before you set off.

Look out for boat noodles served straight from the water, coconut pancakes, mango with sticky rice and the vendors' wide-brimmed straw hats. Away from the main canal, quieter side channels show the wooden houses and orchards that still line the waterways.

Combine it with the railway market

Maeklong Railway Market, roughly half an hour away in Samut Songkhram, is where stallholders fold back their awnings each time a train edges through the market. Train times vary, so your chauffeur will check the day's schedule and plan the visit around it.

Later in the day, Amphawa's weekend floating market comes to life, and Wat Bang Kung, a small temple enclosed by the roots of a banyan tree, is a short drive away. A coconut-sugar farm, where palm sap is boiled down in large woks, makes another genuine local stop.

A full day, one fixed price

Damnoen Saduak is priced on our Eastern Seaboard tier, which includes up to ten hours with the car and chauffeur. That is ample time for the floating market, the railway market, lunch and a further stop, all with the comfort of a cool car waiting between each.

Unlike shared minibus tours, there is no fixed shopping stop and no waiting for other passengers. You decide how long to stay and when to head home. Fuel, tolls and the driver are included; book on WhatsApp with your hotel address and preferred start time.

Families and photographers in particular value the flexibility. If the children are flagging or the light is perfect, you simply adjust the plan with your chauffeur rather than following a guide's flag back to the coach.

Chauffeur's tips

  • Leave Bangkok by 6.30am to reach the canals around 8am, before the crowds.
  • Agree boat duration and route at the pier before you board.
  • Carry small notes; floating vendors rarely have change for large ones.
  • Ask your chauffeur to check Maeklong train times so you see the market fold back.

Questions, answered

Anything else? Our concierge replies on WhatsApp, day or night.

How long does it take to drive to Damnoen Saduak?

Around one and a half to two hours for approximately 100 km, depending on traffic and your pick-up point.

What time should we leave Bangkok?

We recommend around 6.30am, so you are on the water by 8am while the market is at its most authentic.

Are boat fees included?

No. Our fixed price covers the car, chauffeur, fuel and tolls. Boats are hired directly at the market piers.

Can we visit Maeklong Railway Market too?

Yes. It is about half an hour away, and the ten-hour allowance gives plenty of time for both.
