Khao Yai: forest, wine and fresh air

Khao Yai is Bangkok's highland escape: a UNESCO-listed forest with wild elephants and gibbons, ringed by vineyards, farm stays and stylish resorts. The air is cooler, the pace slower and the drive surprisingly easy with a private chauffeur.

≈ 180 km2.5–3 hrsNortheast GatewayFrom 16,000 THB

The route north-east

Your chauffeur leaves Bangkok northwards on Phahonyothin Road, Highway 1, then joins Mittraphap Road, Highway 2, through Saraburi to Pak Chong, the town at the foot of the park. The distance is approximately 180 km and the journey usually takes around two and a half to three hours.

Friday evenings and Sunday afternoons are the busiest times, as Bangkok residents head to their weekend houses. A mid-morning weekday departure is often the smoothest of all.

The national park

Khao Yai National Park forms part of the Dong Phayayen–Khao Yai Forest Complex, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Wild elephants are sometimes seen on the park roads, hornbills and gibbons in the canopy, and waterfalls such as Haew Suwat and Haew Narok are at their most powerful after the rains.

With your own car and driver you can move between viewpoints and trailheads at your own pace, which matters in a park this large. Many visitors hire a local ranger-guide for a walk, and your chauffeur will wait at the trailhead until you return.

The park can be busy with Thai visitors at weekends and on public holidays, particularly around the main waterfalls and viewpoints. A weekday visit, or an early start on a weekend, gives you far quieter trails and a much better chance of spotting wildlife along the roadside.

Vineyards, farms and resorts

Outside the park, Khao Yai has become a leisure destination in its own right. PB Valley and GranMonte are vineyards that offer tours and tastings, Chokchai Farm is a long-established dairy farm popular with families, and Primo Piazza and Palio bring a playful Italian-village style to the hills.

The area's resorts range from forest lodges to design-led pool villas. A chauffeur makes wine tasting relaxed, too: nobody needs to drive afterwards.

Food is part of the experience, from farm cafés serving fresh milk and steak to fine-dining restaurants in the better resorts. Your chauffeur knows the area well and can suggest a lunch stop that suits your group.

Chauffeur's tips

  • Enter the national park early in the morning for the best chance of seeing wildlife.
  • Pack a fleece for evenings between November and February, when the hills are cool.
  • Leave Bangkok before Friday lunchtime to avoid the weekend rush on Mittraphap Road.
  • Wear long trousers and leech socks for forest walks, especially in the rainy season.
