Nakhon Nayok, waterfalls within reach

Nakhon Nayok is the green province Bangkok residents escape to when they want rivers, waterfalls and mountain views without a long journey. It sits at the southern edge of the Khao Yai massif, about two hours from the city, and makes an excellent private day trip.

Out of the city and into the green

Your chauffeur heads north to Rangsit and then east on Highway 305, a road that runs alongside the long, straight Rangsit canal through rice fields towards Nakhon Nayok. The distance is approximately 110 km and the drive takes around two hours, depending on traffic leaving Bangkok's northern suburbs.

As you approach the town, the hills of the Khao Yai range rise ahead, and the air already feels fresher. Roadside stalls along the way sell the province's well-known marian plums, called maprang, in season.

Waterfalls, dam and river

The province's best-known sights lie on the hillside north of town. Sarika Waterfall drops in several tiers through the forest, and Nang Rong Waterfall, with its picnic area and easy paths, is a family favourite. Wang Takrai, a riverside park laid out by a member of the royal family, has shaded lawns along a clear mountain stream.

Khun Dan Prakan Chon Dam holds back a broad reservoir framed by mountains, with a long crest road and viewpoints that are particularly beautiful in the late afternoon. For the more energetic, local operators run white-water rafting and kayaking trips on the Nakhon Nayok River, typically in the rainier months when the water is higher.

Cafés, resorts and the royal academy

Over recent years, Nakhon Nayok has become known for its riverside cafés and small resorts, many with views of the hills, which makes it a favourite for a long, leisurely lunch. The province is also home to the Chulachomklao Royal Military Academy, whose grounds include a museum.

Visiting all of this by public transport is tricky, because the attractions are spread along country roads with little regular service. With your own chauffeur, the day flows easily from waterfall to dam to café.

It is also a gentle introduction to rural Thailand for first-time visitors: orchards, temples and village markets line the back roads, and your chauffeur is happy to stop whenever something catches your eye.

Chauffeur's tips

  • Waterfalls are fullest from around August to November, after the main rains.
  • Bring sandals with grip and a change of clothes if you plan to wade or raft.
  • Leave Bangkok by 8am to make the most of the cooler morning in the hills.
  • Try maprang, the local marian plum, from roadside stalls when it is in season.
