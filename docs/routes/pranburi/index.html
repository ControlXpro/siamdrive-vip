Pranburi, beyond the busy coast

South of Hua Hin, the coast softens into pine-fringed beaches, fishing villages and some of Thailand's most discreet boutique resorts. Pranburi rewards the extra half hour on the road with space, silence and limestone headlands on the horizon.

The route and timing

Your chauffeur follows Rama II Road and then Phetkasem Road, Highway 4, through Phetchaburi and past Hua Hin before turning towards the coast at Pranburi. The distance is roughly 230 km, and the journey takes approximately three and a half hours.

That is long enough to warrant a pause. Most guests stop once, either for coffee near Phetchaburi or for lunch in Hua Hin, before the final, quieter stretch to the resorts strung along the Pak Nam Pran and Khao Kalok shoreline.

What makes Pranburi special

Pranburi has largely avoided high-rise development. Instead you find low-slung villas, beach houses set back behind casuarinas, and small restaurants serving the day's catch. It is a favourite of Bangkok residents who want a private weekend rather than a scene.

Nature is the main attraction. Pranburi Forest Park has a boardwalk through mangroves, and Khao Sam Roi Yot National Park, further south, is home to Phraya Nakhon Cave, where a royal pavilion sits beneath a natural skylight. Sam Phraya Beach inside the park is usually close to deserted on weekdays.

Kite-surfers come for the steady seasonal winds, birdwatchers for the marshes of Sam Roi Yot, and everyone else for long, unhurried lunches overlooking the Gulf. It is the kind of place where an evening plan rarely extends beyond choosing which beach to watch the sunset from.

Planning a longer stay

Pranburi is priced on our long-distance tier. Most guests take a one-way transfer and book the return separately, while some choose the day-trip rate to visit Sam Roi Yot with the chauffeur waiting at the trailhead. The rate table below shows both options.

Because resorts here can be some distance apart and taxis are scarce, it is worth arranging local transport in advance. Message us on WhatsApp with your plans and we will advise what makes sense for your stay.

Why the private car makes sense here

Trains and buses stop in Pranburi town, several kilometres inland from the beach, and the final leg usually means bargaining for a local taxi with all your luggage. A private chauffeur removes that last-mile problem entirely, taking you from your Bangkok doorstep to the resort's own driveway.

Three and a half hours is also a long stretch in a cramped seat. In an Alphard or Vellfire you can recline, sleep or work, and the chauffeur will stop whenever you wish. For couples, the Executive Lounge model makes the journey feel like part of the holiday rather than the price of reaching it.

Chauffeur's tips

  • Phraya Nakhon Cave involves a steep hike; start before 9am and wear proper shoes.
  • Taxis are hard to find in Pranburi, so plan any local outings with your hotel or with us.
  • Leave Bangkok early in the morning to reach the coast in time for lunch by the sea.
  • Bring insect repellent for the mangrove boardwalk and evening dinners outdoors.

How long is the drive to Pranburi?

Approximately three and a half hours for around 230 km, including the stretch past Hua Hin. A short stop along the way is recommended.

Yes. Choose the day-trip rate or ask on WhatsApp, and your chauffeur can take you into the national park and wait while you explore.

Is Pranburi suitable for families?

Very much so. The beaches are calm and uncrowded, and many villas have private pools and gardens.
