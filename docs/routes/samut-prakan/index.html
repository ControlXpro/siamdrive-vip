Samut Prakan, just beyond the city

Samut Prakan begins where Bangkok's south-eastern suburbs meet the Gulf of Thailand. Within an hour of the city centre you find the vast open-air museum of the Ancient City, the towering three-headed elephant of the Erawan Museum and the green, car-free lanes of Bang Krachao.

A short, flexible drive

Samut Prakan lies approximately 30 km from central Bangkok. Depending on where you are going, your chauffeur takes Sukhumvit Road, the Bang Na–Bang Pakong expressway or the expressway network south across the river, and the drive usually takes around 45 minutes to an hour.

The province also surrounds Suvarnabhumi Airport, so its attractions are a convenient way to fill a free day before a late departure, with your luggage safely in the car.

Ancient City and Erawan Museum

The Ancient City, known in Thai as Muang Boran, is a huge outdoor park shaped roughly like the map of Thailand, filled with full-size and scaled replicas of the country's great temples and palaces. It is so large that visitors usually drive or cycle between the sites, which makes a chauffeur particularly useful.

The Erawan Museum is unmistakable from the road: a giant copper-clad three-headed elephant standing on a building that houses an ornate interior of stained glass, sculpture and antiques. It is a short, striking visit that fits easily into any day.

Bang Krachao and the coast

Bang Krachao, often called Bangkok's green lung, is a loop of the Chao Phraya River left largely to orchards, mangroves and raised cycle paths. Sri Nakhon Khuean Khan Park, a public park and botanical garden, is the place to walk among birds and ponds, and the weekend floating market at Bang Nam Phueng is a cheerful local scene.

On the coast, Bang Pu is famous for the flocks of seagulls that winter there, roughly from November to April, and for its seaside pier and promenade. Phra Samut Chedi, a white chedi on the river near the mouth of the Chao Phraya, is the provincial emblem.

Cycling is the best way to explore Bang Krachao's raised paths, and bicycles can be hired near the piers. Your chauffeur can drop you at one end and collect you later, so there is no need to retrace your route.
