Sattahip, the coast beyond Pattaya

Sattahip lies south of Pattaya, where the hotel towers give way to naval bases, fishing villages and some of the cleanest beaches on the Eastern Seaboard. It is also next door to U-Tapao Airport, which makes it a practical base for travellers flying in and out of the east.

Getting there

Your chauffeur follows Motorway 7 from Bangkok towards Pattaya, then continues south on the motorway or Sukhumvit Road, Highway 3, past Jomtien and Na Jomtien to Sattahip. For approximately 180 km, allow around two and a half hours.

The final stretch is quieter than central Pattaya and passes Bang Saray, a small fishing village with good seafood restaurants on the water. It is a natural place to pause for lunch if you set off in the morning.

Because Sattahip is beyond the busiest part of Pattaya, your chauffeur will usually stay on the motorway as long as possible and avoid the slow city-centre stretch of Sukhumvit Road. That small detail can save a surprising amount of time on a Friday or during the high season.

A navy town with fine beaches

Sattahip is home to the Royal Thai Navy's main base, and several of its best beaches, such as Hat Sai Kaew and Nang Ram, are administered by the navy. They are cleaner and calmer than Pattaya's, though access rules apply and foreign visitors may need to show identification or travel with a Thai companion at some beaches.

Samae San, at the southern tip of Chonburi province, is a fishing community with boats to nearby islands for snorkelling and a growing number of seafood restaurants overlooking the water.

The pace here is decidedly local: naval families, fishing boats and simple beachfront restaurants rather than bars and beach clubs. That is exactly its appeal for travellers who want the sea without the scene.

Gardens, viewpoints and golf

Nong Nooch Tropical Garden, one of the region's best-known attractions, lies within Sattahip district, with themed gardens, a large orchid collection and cultural shows. Nearby, Khao Chi Chan displays a huge Buddha image inlaid in gold on a limestone cliff, an impressive sight in the late afternoon light.

Several of the Eastern Seaboard's well-regarded golf courses sit between Pattaya and Sattahip, so this route is popular with golfing groups who prefer staying away from the city centre.

Chauffeur's tips

  • Carry your passport for navy-administered beaches, where identification may be checked.
  • Visit Khao Chi Chan in late afternoon, when the gold image catches the light.
  • Boats to the Samae San islands depend on sea conditions; mornings are usually calmer.
  • If flying from U-Tapao, a Sattahip hotel the night before keeps the airport run short.
